# Aqualeaf Brand & Communications Guideline (2026 Edition)

Welcome to the Aqualeaf editorial and brand governance standard. All creators, agency partners, and copywriters producing customer-facing communications for Aqualeaf must strictly follow these core directives. Our brand reputation relies on transparent, evidence-first storytelling that empowers everyday consumers to choose sustainable hydration.

## 1. Product Claims and Substantiation
- **Superlative Claims:** Never use unverified superlatives or number one rankings without approved survey backing. Unsubstantiated claims such as "the best bottle in India" or "No.1 rated hydration gear" harm brand credibility and violate advertising standards.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** All numeric figures, percentages, and duration claims must cite approved in-date facts. Do not round up test hours or exaggerate bottle thermal retention capabilities.
- **Social Impact Claims:** Every claim regarding charitable donations or environmental initiatives must name the verified partner organization and specify the exact audited contribution percentage.
- **Health Claims:** Do not suggest medicinal remedies or therapeutic health cures from drinking water from stainless steel containers.

## 2. Voice and Tone Standards
- **Direct Engagement:** Adopt an encouraging, active voice that speaks directly to the reader using "you" rather than impersonal third-person prose. Keep our copy warm, relatable, and optimistic.
- **Ethical Urgency:** Avoid artificial urgency, fear triggers, or aggressive sales rhetoric like "buy immediately" or "act before it's too late". Inspire conscious lifestyle changes rather than panic purchasing.

## 3. Product Terminology
- **Product Nomenclature:** Always refer to the product as an insulated steel bottle and avoid outdated descriptors like metal flask or plastic thermos.
- **Trademark Spelling:** Maintain consistent capitalization and spelling for all trademarked bottle models, particularly Aqualeaf Flow and Aqualeaf Classic. Never separate or hyphenate the brand name.

## 4. Digital Accessibility
- **Sentence Clarity:** Keep sentences concise with fewer than twenty-five words to maximize readability across diverse consumer demographics.
- **Graphic Accessibility:** Provide detailed alternative text descriptions for all digital product imagery and social media campaign graphics. Screen reader users must be able to understand the visual message completely.
